excerpt: '**The Meaning of <i>eya</i> and Moods** In this paper, I give the semantics and pragmatic properties of <i>-eya</i> conditional. I claim that a conditional with <i>-eya</i> means that the antecedent clause is a downward-bound sufficient condition for the consequent clause. And in a conditional with <i>-eya</i>, the consequent clause is a topic. From this we can explain why an <i>-eya</i> conditional does not allow other moods than the declarative and interrogative. This also explains why a conditional with <i>-eya</i> does not allow an interrogative with a <i>wh</i>-phrase in the consequent clause. A conditional with <i>-eya</i> does not allow a modal operator used to ask about the hearer’s willingness to accept the speaker’s offering. An <i>-eya</i> conditional can be used with a concessive meaning, but its semantics is the same as that of a normal conditional use. The only difference is the expected result determined by the context, which is reflected in the ordering of scalar alternaives for the antecedent clause.'

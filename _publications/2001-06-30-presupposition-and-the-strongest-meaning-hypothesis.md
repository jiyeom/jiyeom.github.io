excerpt: 'In this paper, I claim that the Strongest Meaning Hypothesis explains why presuppositions tendd to be projected. Presuppositions can be regarded as a means of conveying information, not as requirements for interpretation. When a sentence contains operators, presuppositions will have various scopes with respect to the operators depending on how far they are projected from their original positions. Then the interpretation of the sentence yields a set of readings, and the reading in which a presupposition is projected is stronger than the one in which it is not. In deriving a set of readings, I follow the principle of compositionality. This blocks some impossible readdings which other theiries may predict. Finally, I discuss disjunctive sentences, which show asymmetry in anaphora and symmetry in presupposition projection.'

This is part of my Ph.D. thesis. Presuppositions are projected according to the strongest meaning hypothesis. This implies that presuppositions are not pragmatic. Presupposition projection is a scope phenomenon. Pragmatics selects one of the readings as the actual meaning of the sentence in the context.

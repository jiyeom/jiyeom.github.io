excerpt: "**The Comparative Study of the Modalities of '-keyss' and '-(u)l kes' in Korean** In this paper I propose the semantics of two modality markers in Korean, <i>keyss</i> and <i>(u)l kes</i>. I compare the two modality markers with respect to some properties. First, <i>keyss</i> is used to express logical necessity while <i>(u)l kes</i> can be used to express a simple prediction as well. Second, keyss expresses some logical conclusion from the speaker’s own information state without claiming it is true. On the other hand, <i>(u)l kes</i> expresses the claim that the speaker’s prediction will be true. Third, the prediction of <i>keyss</i> is non-monotonic because it can be reversed without being inconsistent. However, that of <i>(u)l kes</i> cannot. Fourth, <i>(u)l kes</i> can be used freely in epistemic conditionals, but <i>keyss</i> cannot. Finally, when <i>keyss</i> is used, the prediction cannot be repeated. The prediction from the use of <i>(u)l kes</i> can be repeated. To account for these diﬀerences, I propose that <i>keyss</i> is used when the speaker makes a purely logical presumption based on his/her own information state, and that <i>(u)l kes</i> is used to make a prediction which is asserted to be true. This proposal accounts for all the diﬀerences of the two modality markers."

Previous analyses deal with the two modality markers only based on intuitions when there are some categories of meanings related to modality established in semantics. In this paper, I discuss the two markers more systematically.

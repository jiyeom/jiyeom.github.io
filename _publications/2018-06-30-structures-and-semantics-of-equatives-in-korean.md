excerpt: "In this paper, I discuss equative constructions with <i>mankhum</i>. They are different from comparatives in three ways. First, in a clausal comparison construction, <i>pota</i> is always preceded by a <i>kes</i>-phrase, while <i>mankhum</i> is preceded by an adnominal clause. Second, <i>pota</i> can be used with a differential degree, while <i>mankhum</i> cannot. Third, <i>mankhum</i> can be used with a clause without a gradable verb, still denoting a degree, but <i>pota</i> cannot. These properties are related to the observation that in Korean, when an adnominal clause denotes a property of degrees, a Deg(ree )P(hrase) shows the property of a syntactic movement of an adjunct to the Spec of a CP. This motivates the idea that a degree is related to a situation. I also deal with quantificational DegP. An adnominal clause that denotes a degree with no gradable verb is a case of a coerced relative clause. A coerced relative clause is normally analyzed by inserting a contextually given relation between a situation described by the clause and the denotation of the head noun. This further motivates the adjunct analysis of a DegP."

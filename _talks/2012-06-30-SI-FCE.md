[SLIDE](https://jiyeom.github.io/files/FCvsSI4.pdf) In this paper I compare scalar implicatures (SIs) and free choice (FC) effects in various respects, and claim that FC effects are not SIs. Intuitively, SIs involve the negation operator, while FC effects involve existential quantification. This indicates that their sources are different. This is empirically supported that in down-ward entailing (DE) contexts standard SIs do not arise. But FC effects are observed in some DE contexts. This means that FC effects do not come from monotonicity or scalarity. I attribute FC effects to the condition that every expression must make a meaning contribution to the meaning of the sentence it occurs independently of the other expressions. This leads to the existence of some semantic entities in a domain of a quantifier, normally the quantifier which has immediately wide scope over a disjunction structure. Therefore the same effects are expressed from uses of other expressions. Thus I also discuss similar effects from conjunction structures and conditionals.
